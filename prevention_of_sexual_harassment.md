# Prevention of Sexual Harassment at the Workplace (POSH)

## Question 1: What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

**Answer:**

Sexual harassment is any **unwelcome behaviour of a sexual nature**. It can be physical, verbal, non-verbal, or online. Examples include:

1. **Unwelcome advances:** Unwanted sexual advances, propositions, requests for sexual favours, or repeated unwanted invitations for dates or outings.
2. **Sexual comments and jokes:** Remarks about a person's body or appearance, sexual jokes, innuendos, suggestive comments, or offensive remarks about someone's personal or sexual life.
3. **Inappropriate gestures:** Eve-teasing, whistling, leering, staring at someone's body, or making sexually suggestive gestures.
4. **Unwanted physical contact:** Touching, brushing against someone's body, stroking their hair, pinching, patting, grabbing, groping, kissing, or any other unwanted physical contact.
5. **Sexually explicit material:** Displaying or sharing sexually suggestive pictures, posters, videos, or other explicit material.
6. **Offensive communication:** Sending sexually offensive emails, messages, images, or voicemails, making inappropriate calls, or leaving lewd messages.
7. **Abuse of authority:** Offering benefits or favourable treatment in exchange for sexual favours, or threatening negative consequences when such advances are rejected.
8. **Hostile work environment:** Repeated or serious unwelcome sexual conduct that makes the workplace intimidating, humiliating, offensive, or unsafe.

Workplace policies may also prohibit gender-based insults, sexist remarks, stereotyping, unequal work assignments, or denying career opportunities because of a person's gender. Such conduct is unacceptable and may contribute to a hostile or discriminatory workplace. Whether a particular incident legally qualifies as sexual harassment depends on its facts and circumstances.

Sexual harassment can occur between people of the same gender or different genders. Workplace protections and reporting procedures may cover offices, client locations, work-related travel, and other work-connected settings, depending on the applicable policy and law.

## Question 2: What would you do if you faced or witnessed any incident or repeated incidents of such behaviour?

**Answer:**

If I faced or witnessed sexual harassment, I would take the following steps:

1. **Prioritise safety:** Move to a safe place and seek immediate help if there is any threat. If it is safe, clearly tell the person that the behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop. Confrontation is not compulsory if it could put someone at risk.
2. **Document the incident:** Record the date, time, location, what happened, names of possible witnesses, and how I responded. Preserve relevant emails, messages, screenshots, or other evidence.
3. **Report through the proper channel:** Report the incident to the organisation's Internal Committee (IC/ICC), designated PoSH contact, or HR, following the official complaint procedure. Where appropriate, submit the complaint in writing and keep a copy.
4. **Support the affected person:** If I witness an incident, I would listen without blaming or judging the person, ask what support they need, and offer to provide an honest account of what I observed.
5. **Seek urgent assistance when necessary:** If the incident involves assault, immediate danger, or a serious threat, I would contact the appropriate emergency services or police, in addition to using the workplace reporting process.
6. **Maintain confidentiality and avoid retaliation:** I would not spread rumours or share details unnecessarily. I would cooperate with the authorised investigation and avoid intimidating, blaming, or retaliating against anyone involved.
7. **Follow up appropriately:** I would ask about the next steps in the reporting process and seek support from the designated workplace authority if the behaviour continues or the complaint is not addressed.

A workplace should have a clear process for receiving and addressing complaints, including an Internal Committee where required by law. Complaints should be handled fairly, sensitively, and confidentially, and appropriate action should be taken based on the findings.
