# Listening and Active Communication

## Question 1: What are the steps or strategies for active listening?

**Answer:**

1.  **Give your full attention:** Stop other activities, put away
    distractions, and focus on the speaker.
2.  **Maintain appropriate eye contact:** Show that you are interested
    without staring or making the other person uncomfortable.
3.  **Avoid interrupting:** Allow the speaker to complete their thoughts
    before responding.
4.  **Observe non-verbal signals:** Pay attention to facial expressions,
    gestures, posture, and tone of voice.
5.  **Ask relevant questions:** Ask open-ended or clarifying questions
    when something is unclear.
6.  **Paraphrase the message:** Repeat the main idea in your own words
    to confirm that you have understood it correctly.
7.  **Acknowledge feelings:** Recognise the speaker's emotions and
    respond with empathy instead of immediately judging or giving
    advice.
8.  **Summarise important points:** Briefly review the key information
    and confirm that nothing important has been missed.
9.  **Respond thoughtfully:** Give a relevant response based on what the
    speaker actually said, rather than preparing an answer while they
    are still talking.

## Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of reflective listening?

**Answer:**

Fisher's model explains reflective listening through four important
practices:

1.  **Respect:** Respect the speaker's opinions and feelings, even when
    they are different from mine. I should listen without judging or
    making assumptions.
2.  **Real-time attention:** Concentrate on what the person is saying at
    that moment. I should avoid distractions and not prepare my response
    before the speaker has finished.
3.  **Relate:** Try to understand the situation from the speaker's point
    of view. I should show empathy through suitable words, facial
    expressions, and body language.
4.  **Reflect:** Repeat the important meaning of the message in my own
    words. I should reflect the speaker's key points and feelings,
    clarify anything confusing, and summarise the discussion to check my
    understanding.

These practices help prevent misunderstandings and make the speaker feel
heard and respected.

## Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

**Answer:**

The main obstacles I sometimes face while listening are:

1.  **Distractions:** Background noise, mobile notifications, and other
    activities can reduce my concentration.
2.  **Thinking about my response:** Sometimes I start preparing what I
    want to say before the other person has finished speaking.
3.  **Assumptions:** I may think I already know what the speaker is
    going to say and stop paying proper attention.
4.  **Lack of patience:** During long explanations, I may lose focus or
    become impatient.
5.  **Overthinking:** I sometimes focus on my own thoughts instead of
    understanding the speaker's complete message.
6.  **Technical unfamiliarity:** In technical discussions, unfamiliar
    terms or concepts can make it difficult to follow the entire
    conversation.
7.  **Emotional reactions:** If I disagree with something, I may focus
    on that disagreement instead of listening to the remaining points.

## Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

**Answer:**

I can improve my listening by following these practices:

1.  Keep my phone away and avoid unnecessary distractions during
    conversations.
2.  Allow the speaker to finish without interrupting.
3.  Focus on understanding the message instead of preparing my reply.
4.  Ask questions whenever I do not understand a point.
5.  Take short notes during important meetings and technical
    discussions.
6.  Repeat or summarise important information to confirm that I
    understood it correctly.
7.  Pay attention to the speaker's tone, facial expressions, and body
    language.
8.  Keep an open mind and avoid judging the speaker too quickly.
9.  Practise active listening in everyday conversations so that it
    becomes a regular habit.

## Question 5: When do you switch to a passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

**Answer:**

I sometimes use passive communication when I am speaking with someone
senior, when I want to avoid an argument, or when I am not confident
about expressing my opinion. For example, during a group discussion, I
may agree with a decision even when I have a different suggestion
because I do not want to create disagreement.

This behaviour can prevent others from understanding my actual thoughts
and needs. I should learn to express my opinions respectfully instead of
remaining silent just to avoid conflict.

## Question 6: When do you switch into aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

**Answer:**

I may become aggressive when I am frustrated, under pressure, or feel
that someone is repeatedly ignoring my point. In such situations, I
might speak too loudly, interrupt the other person, or respond without
thinking carefully.

This communication style can hurt others and create unnecessary
conflicts. I should control my emotions, listen to the other person's
point of view, and explain my disagreement calmly.

## Question 7: When do you switch into passive-aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

**Answer:**

I may use passive-aggressive communication when I feel disappointed or
irritated but do not express my feelings directly. For example, instead
of explaining why I am upset, I might use sarcasm, give short replies,
avoid a conversation, or make indirect comments.

This behaviour does not solve the actual problem and can create
confusion or damage relationships. I should communicate the reason for
my frustration honestly and respectfully rather than expecting others to
guess what I feel.

## Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?

**Answer:**

I can make my communication more assertive by expressing my thoughts,
feelings, and needs clearly while respecting the opinions and rights of
others. I can apply the following steps:

1.  **Understand my feelings:** Identify what I am feeling before
    responding. This helps me communicate clearly instead of reacting
    emotionally.
2.  **Use clear and respectful language:** Say what I mean directly
    without blaming, insulting, or disrespecting anyone.
3.  **Use "I" statements:** Explain how a situation affects me. For
    example, instead of saying, "You never listen to me," I can say, "I
    feel ignored when I am interrupted."
4.  **Express my opinions confidently:** Share my ideas and suggestions,
    even when they are different from the opinions of others.
5.  **Learn to say no politely:** If I cannot take on additional work, I
    should explain my situation honestly instead of agreeing and
    becoming frustrated later.
6.  **Listen to the other person's perspective:** Assertive
    communication is not about forcing my opinion. I should give others
    the same respect that I expect from them.
7.  **Address problems early:** If someone's behaviour bothers me, I
    should discuss it calmly instead of keeping my frustration inside
    until it becomes a bigger issue.
8.  **Control my tone and body language:** Speak calmly, maintain
    appropriate eye contact, and use confident but non-threatening body
    language.
9.  **Accept disagreement:** Understand that people may have different
    opinions. I can disagree respectfully without becoming passive or
    aggressive.
10. **Practise regularly:** Apply these habits in daily conversations,
    group discussions, meetings, and professional situations.

**Example:**

If a team member repeatedly assigns me additional tasks when I already
have a full workload, I can say:

"I can help with this task, but I am currently working on two important
assignments. Could we discuss the priorities and decide which task
should be completed first?"

This response communicates my situation clearly, sets a reasonable
boundary, and remains respectful.
