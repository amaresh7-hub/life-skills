## 2. Tiny Habits by BJ Fogg – Core Message

**Video:** [Tiny Habits – Core Message](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_8e-6ZHKLs)

### Question 2: How can you use B = MAP to make new habits easier? What are M, A and P?

According to BJ Fogg, a behaviour happens when three things come together at the same time. This is called **B = MAP**.

- **M – Motivation:** It is the desire or willingness to do something.
- **A – Ability:** It means how easy or difficult the action is to complete.
- **P – Prompt:** It is a reminder or signal that tells us to do the action.

For example, if I want to develop a habit of reading, I can keep a book on my study table as a reminder (Prompt), start with just one page so it is easy (Ability), and remind myself that reading will improve my knowledge (Motivation).

To build a habit, I should not depend only on motivation. I should make the action simple and connect it to a clear reminder.

### Question 3: Why is it important to "Shine" or celebrate after each successful completion of a habit?

Celebrating after completing a habit gives us a positive feeling of success. BJ Fogg calls this feeling **Shine**. When we celebrate, our brain connects the action with a good feeling, making us more likely to repeat it.

For example, after completing my daily study target, I can smile and say, "Good job!" Even if I study for only five minutes, I should appreciate the effort. The celebration does not need to be big. A small moment of happiness is enough.

I feel this is important because we usually wait for big results before appreciating ourselves. But celebrating small achievements helps us stay positive and consistent. In simple words, when we feel good after doing something, we are more likely to do it again.

## 4. Book Summary of Atomic Habits

**Video:** [Atomic Habits – Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YT7tQzmGRLA)

### Question 5: What is the book's perspective about Identity?

According to *Atomic Habits* by James Clear, long-term habit change starts with changing our identity. Instead of focusing only on what we want to achieve, we should focus on the type of person we want to become.

For example, instead of saying, "I want to solve more coding problems," I can say, "I am a person who practises coding every day." Every time I practise, I give myself proof that I am becoming that kind of person.

The main idea is that our habits help shape our identity. Small actions repeated every day slowly build the person we want to become.

### Question 6: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do.

James Clear explains that we should reduce the effort needed to perform a good habit. If a habit is easy to start, we are more likely to do it regularly.

Some ways to make a habit easier are:

- Keep the things needed for the habit ready in advance.
- Start with a very small action.
- Prepare your environment so that there are fewer distractions.
- Follow the two-minute rule: make the starting version of a habit take less than two minutes.

For example, if I want to practise coding every day, I can keep my laptop ready, open my code editor and decide which problem I will solve beforehand. This saves time and makes it easier to begin.

### Question 7: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do.

To reduce a bad habit, James Clear suggests increasing the effort required to perform it. If something takes more effort to access, we are less likely to do it automatically.

Some ways to make a bad habit harder are:

- Remove unnecessary apps from the home screen.
- Keep the phone away while studying.
- Turn off notifications that are not important.
- Log out of distracting websites or apps.
- Add extra steps before doing something that wastes time.

For example, if I spend too much time scrolling on my phone, I can keep it in another room while studying and turn off social media notifications. This creates distance between me and the habit and helps me avoid doing it without thinking.

## 5. Reflection

### Question 8: Pick one habit that you would like to do more of. What steps can you take to make the cue obvious, the habit attractive or easy, and the response satisfying?

The habit I would like to develop is **practising coding every day**. As a computer science student, regular practice will help me improve my problem-solving skills.

I can follow these steps:

- **Make the cue obvious:** After finishing my breakfast, I will sit at my study table and open my code editor.
- **Make it attractive:** I will choose coding problems based on topics I enjoy and track my daily progress.
- **Make it easy:** I will start by solving at least one small problem or practising for just five minutes. I can increase the time gradually.
- **Make it satisfying:** After completing the practice, I will mark it as completed in a habit tracker and appreciate my effort.

My goal is not to solve many problems in one day. My goal is to practise consistently and improve little by little.

### Question 9: Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of. What steps can you take to make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, or the response unsatisfying?

The habit I would like to reduce is **unnecessary mobile phone usage, especially scrolling through social media while studying**. It wastes my time and breaks my concentration.

I can follow these steps:

- **Make the cue invisible:** Keep my phone away from my study table and turn off unnecessary notifications.
- **Make it unattractive:** Remind myself that excessive scrolling takes time away from my learning and daily goals.
- **Make it hard:** Keep the phone in another room while studying and use app limits to control screen time.
- **Make it unsatisfying:** Track the time I waste on social media and notice how it affects my study schedule.

I do not expect to stop this habit in one day. I will try to reduce my screen time gradually and use that time for more useful activities.
